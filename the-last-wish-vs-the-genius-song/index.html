A closer look

Do you want a ritual with reading material or a single listening session?

These are not two different media. They are two variations on short-form manifestation audio, so the framing and the package matter more than the runtime.

01 / THE LAST WISH

A story leads the listening.

The Last Wish opens with a story about the pineal gland as a spiritual center and presents its short audio as a way to activate it and attract abundance. The listening itself is brief; most of the package is the story around it and the three bonus guides.

No individual creator is named. The seller presents itself as Soul Mountain, and the page carries standard disclaimers: its statements are not medical advice, results are not guaranteed, and ClickBank is the retailer of record.

Research-sounding language can feel authoritative, especially when universities are named. Treat that feeling as part of the sales presentation rather than evidence the audio does what is claimed.

  • Best for: people who like a ritual-plus-reading package around a short listen.
  • What is visible: an audio with three bonus guides and pineal-themed framing.
  • What to check: the current price, what the bonuses actually contain, refund terms, support, and billing terms.

02 / THE GENIUS SONG

The session leads the listening.

The Genius Song keeps it simple: one 12-minute audio, said to encourage Theta brainwave activity, made by an unnamed team of sound engineers and neuroscientists. There are no bonuses and no named personality behind it.

One practical note: the marketplace offer is called The Genius Song, but the sales page you land on brands the product as The Genius Wave. It is the same offer, and the page states a 90-day money-back guarantee, terms apply.

Brainwave language sounds technical, but a defined session length and a stated refund window are the concrete parts of this offer. The claims about what the listening produces remain seller marketing.

  • Best for: people who want one defined session and a stated refund window.
  • What is visible: a single 12-minute audio with brainwave framing.
  • What to check: the current price, the guarantee terms, what counts as a valid refund, support, and billing terms.

THE SIMPLE CHOICE

Choose the package shape you will actually use.

Choose The Last Wish if you want a short listen wrapped in a ritual, with guides to read alongside it. Choose The Genius Song if you want a single defined session and the simplest possible purchase.

The important similarity is just as clear: both use scientific-sounding language about the brain. Neither should be used as a medical treatment, a financial plan, or a substitute for professional guidance.

Before paying, ask what you actually receive, whether the price is one-time or recurring, how refunds work, and whether the product matches the experience you expected.

BEFORE YOU CLICK

A buyer checklist for audio offers.

A short audio can carry large claims. Slow the sequence down before entering payment details or relying on scientific-sounding language.

1. Notice the mechanismAsk what the audio actually does: you listen. Everything else is a claim about the listening.
2. Read the termsCheck price, billing cadence, refunds, support, and cancellation.
3. Protect your informationConsider what email or other personal data the flow requests.
4. Keep the role clearUse audio for reflection or relaxation, not medical treatment or financial certainty.

What is The Last Wish?

The Last Wish is a short manifestation audio from the seller Soul Mountain, framed around pineal-gland activation and attracting abundance. The package includes the audio plus three bonus guides. Research claims on the sales page are seller marketing, not verified facts.

What is The Genius Song, and why does its sales page say The Genius Wave?

Which audio experience is better?

What should I check before buying?
